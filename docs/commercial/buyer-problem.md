# Buyer Problem

## Top Buyer Pains

- AI-assisted workflows are difficult to audit end to end.
- Business teams cannot prove what happened across distributed services.
- Logs are too technical for audit, risk, and operations review.
- Document automation often lacks correlation-level evidence.
- Payment workflows need replay safety, idempotency, and traceability.
- Tenant isolation is often assumed instead of demonstrated.
- Compliance teams need evidence packages, not raw service logs.

## Cost Of Doing Nothing

- Audit preparation remains slow, manual, and inconsistent.
- Compliance teams depend on engineers to reconstruct workflow history.
- AI workflow approvals stall because leaders cannot see reliable control evidence.
- Payment and document incidents take longer to investigate.
- Customer trust erodes when teams cannot explain automated decisions or workflow outcomes.
- Regulated AI initiatives remain limited to low-risk use cases.

## Operational Risks

- Fragmented evidence across services, logs, databases, and message streams
- Unclear ownership when workflow steps fail
- Slow incident response for tenant-specific or correlation-specific issues
- Manual reconciliation across document, knowledge, payment, and compliance systems
- Limited executive visibility into whether regulated workflows are ready, incomplete, or under review

## Compliance Risks

- Incomplete audit trails
- Evidence that is too technical for compliance review
- Inability to prove control execution by tenant or correlation
- Weak record of system participation across a workflow
- Inconsistent retention and reporting practices

## AI Governance Risks

- AI-generated answers without linked workflow evidence
- Inability to connect AI outputs to documents, payments, and downstream actions
- Limited proof that AI-assisted workflows followed required controls
- Difficulty reviewing model-assisted business actions in operational context

## Payment And Document Workflow Risks

- Duplicate payment requests without clear replay behavior
- Missing or inconsistent idempotency records
- Document extraction results disconnected from downstream decisions
- Payment completion evidence separated from knowledge and document evidence
- Tenant-scoped activity mixed into generic logs or dashboards
